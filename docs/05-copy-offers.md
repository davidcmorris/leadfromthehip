## Offer 1 — The Private 360 {#private-360}

**Price:** [PRICE — brief suggests $450–750]
**Duration:** About three weeks end to end
**Includes:** Rater protocol · administration · themed report · 45-minute readout

**The pitch:**

Almost every 360 you have ever been given was commissioned by your employer. They
chose the instrument, the timing, and often the raters. The results went into a system
you don't control, at a moment that suited someone else's calendar.

There is no reason it has to work that way. A 360 is only a corporate instrument
because companies were the ones with the budget.

This one is yours. You commission it, you nominate the raters, you pick the moment.
I administer it, aggregate the responses, and theme the comments. You never see raw
responses and neither does anyone else — a four-rater minimum per group protects
anonymity, and I tell your raters exactly that when I invite them.

**What you get:**
- A structured behavioral instrument, not a personality test
- Four to six raters you nominate, across the groups you care about
- Themed verbatim comments — never quoted in a way that identifies a rater
- A 45-minute readout focused on what to do, not on what the numbers mean
- A file that exists in exactly one place: with you

**Who it's for:** Anyone who suspects the people around them are being kind rather
than accurate. If you are hard to rattle and pleasant to work for, you are probably
getting less honest feedback than you think.

## Offer 2 — Terrain & Route {#terrain-route}

**Price:** [PRICE — brief suggests $1,800–3,000]
**Duration:** Ninety days
**Includes:** Assessment battery · 90-minute debrief · written route · 90-day re-measure

**The pitch:**

The full arc, and the reason the practice exists.

We start by mapping your terrain — the dispositions that make some leadership
behaviors cheap for you and others permanently expensive. Then we find the two or
three beliefs that fire when you are under load, because those are what actually drive
your behavior on a bad Thursday, not your intentions on a good Monday.

Then you get a route: two or three reps, each with a trigger, a slot, and a script,
designed around your terrain rather than in spite of it. Not more discipline. Better
scaffolding.

Ninety days later we measure. Not the personality — that hasn't moved and it isn't
supposed to. The behavior, rated by the people who work for you, compared to where
you started.

## Section — What I won't do

**Lede:** Worth saying plainly, since a lot of this field doesn't.

- **I won't sell you a type.** You are not a color, a letter, or an animal. The
  assessment is a starting point for designing behavior, not an identity.
- **I won't promise personality change.** Dispositions are stable across adulthood.
  Anyone telling you otherwise is selling something. Behavior is trainable; that is
  what we work on.
- **I won't provide therapy.** This is behavioral coaching that borrows a cognitive
  framework. If something clinical surfaces I will say so and refer you on.
- **I won't report on you to anyone.** Not to your manager, not to your HR team, not
  in aggregate. If your employer is paying, we agree the boundary in writing first.

## FAQ

**How much of my time does this take?**
The assessment is about an hour. The debrief is ninety minutes. After that the reps
themselves are designed to cost under five minutes a week — if a rep needs a
recurring block on your calendar, it has been designed wrong.

**Does my employer find out?**
Not from me. Everything is commissioned by you and delivered to you. If your company
is paying, we agree in writing beforehand exactly what they receive — usually
confirmation that the engagement happened, and nothing else.
