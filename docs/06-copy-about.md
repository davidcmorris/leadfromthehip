## Section — The short version

[DAVID — three or four paragraphs. The structure that works:]

**Paragraph 1 — What you actually do.** Your background in plain language. Not a CV
summary; the version you'd say out loud to someone at a table.

**Paragraph 2 — The specific frustration that produced this practice.** You have sat
through or delivered assessment debriefs that were accurate and inert. Name a real
moment if you have one — a specific debrief, a specific client, a specific report that
went in a drawer. Concrete beats abstract by a wide margin here.

**Paragraph 3 — What you concluded.** That the missing piece was never better
assessment. It was the translation into behavior, plus the discipline to come back and
measure. Say it plainly.

**Paragraph 4 — Why the Stoics and the cognitive framework.** Brief. The interesting
fact does the work: Ellis built REBT explicitly on Epictetus, and a derailing pattern
under stress is functionally a rigid belief firing. You didn't fuse two unrelated
things; you noticed they were already related.
