## Section 01 — Why the last thing you tried didn't stick

**Lede:** You have probably already done at least one of these. Here is why none of
them changed your Thursday afternoon.

Three cards:

**The report that named you accurately.**
It was well-built and it was right about you. You nodded. It stung a little. Then it
went in a drawer, because a description of who you are is not an instruction for what
to do on Thursday.

**The advice that costs everyone the same.**
"Run weekly one-to-ones." "Assume positive intent." Fine advice, priced at zero — as
though a structured weekly meeting costs a naturally organized person and a naturally
spontaneous one the same amount. It doesn't, and pretending otherwise is why the
resolution lasted nine days.

**The feedback that arrived once a year.**
Aggregated, anonymized, delivered in a form your company chose, about a period you
can no longer remember. You didn't pick the raters. You never saw whether anything
you changed afterward registered with anyone.

**Closing claim (pull quote):** Every one of those stops in the same place — at
insight. Nothing in the standard playbook is built to change what you actually do
when you're tired and it's late in the week.

## Section 02 — The method {#method}

**Lede:** Four stages. The first two are diagnosis and take about a week. The third
is where the work is. The fourth is the part almost nobody sells.

Four-stage strip:

**01 · Terrain**
Your stable dispositions, read properly. Not a label and not a type — a map of where
routines will come cheap to you and where they will always cost.
*Question: What comes free to you, and what never will?*

**02 · Load**
What this role, this team, and this moment are actually asking of you. Terrain barely
moves. Load changes with every promotion and reorg.
*Question: What is this job demanding right now?*

**03 · Route**
Two or three routines with a named trigger, a fixed slot, and a script — designed to
work with your terrain rather than against it. Not more discipline. Better scaffolding.
*Question: What runs on Tuesday at nine without willpower?*

**04 · Evidence**
Ninety days later, the behavior gets re-measured by the people who work for you. Not
the personality — the behavior. Dated, scored, compared to where you started.
*Question: Did anything actually change?*

## Section 03 — What a rep actually looks like

**Lede:** Not a principle. Not a mindset. A specific thing you do, triggered by a
specific moment, that takes under a minute and requires no change to your character.

Render one `RepCard` with these fields:

- **Pattern** — The Interrogator
- **Trigger** — Any message that lands as a slight, after 3 p.m.
- **The rep** — Write the reply in full. Don't send it. Reread at 9 a.m. and send
  whatever still holds up.
- **What it disputes** — "If I don't respond now, they'll read it as weakness."
- **Anchor** — *"Men are disturbed not by things, but by the views which they take
  of them."* — Epictetus
- **Evidence** — Rater item: "When I raise a problem, I can predict how they'll react."

**Below the card:** Thirty seconds of process. No new personality required. That is
the entire design principle — the rep has to be cheap enough that you still run it on
the worst week of the quarter.

## Section 04 — Who this is for

**Lede:** Two honest lists.

**This works if you:**
- Lead a team and have for at least a year
- Have already done a personality assessment and found it interesting but inert
- Are more interested in what to do on Tuesday than in what type you are
- Would rather have a number at ninety days than a good feeling at the end of a session

**This is not for you if you:**
- Want a certification, a framework to roll out, or a team offsite
- Are looking for a diagnosis of someone else on your team
- Need clinical support — that is a different profession and I will say so and refer
