# Positioning — the short version

Full brief: https://claude.ai/code/artifact/4482b009-3b71-4730-a79b-4dc11ca030c5

## Who this is for

Anyone who leads a team. Not HR, not L&D buyers, not procurement. The individual
leader is the customer and the payer.

## The gap in the market

Three mature categories serve this buyer and all three stop in the same place:

- **Personality assessment** — rigorous, well-normed, sold as an event. Ends at insight.
- **Habit and routine content** — prescribes the same behavior to every leader at the
  same imaginary price. Ignores who is doing it.
- **360s and engagement surveys** — bought by the company, delivered as a ritual. The
  leader picks no raters, owns no data, gets no follow-up.

## The wedge

**Behavioral cost.** The same leadership behavior costs different leaders wildly
different amounts. A structured weekly 1:1 is nearly free for one person and genuinely
expensive for another. The advice industry prices every behavior at zero.

So: don't prescribe different behaviors. Prescribe different **scaffolding** for the
same behavior, chosen by profile.

## The method

Assessment identifies the disposition. A cognitive framework (REBT, which descends
directly from the Stoics) surfaces the rigid belief that fires under pressure. The
intervention is a **rep** — a specific behavior with a trigger, a script, and a fixed
slot — rather than a realization. Ninety days later the behavior is re-measured by the
people who work for them.

Four stages, named publicly as: **Terrain → Load → Route → Evidence.**

## The differentiator that has commercial teeth

Almost nobody sells the follow-up measurement. Vendors sell the report; coaches sell
hours. The ninety-day behavioral re-measure is priced into the engagement, never sold
as a bolt-on.

## Brand name logic — important for copy

"Lead from the hip" is not a contradiction of the thesis, it is the thesis. Every
leader already leads from the hip: under pressure you fall back on your defaults, not
your training. The work is not to stop doing that. It is to **know what your hip does**,
and to build the two or three routines that catch it.

That reading should be established early on the home page. It is the site's single
best paragraph and it earns the domain name.

## Voice

Direct, specific, unsentimental. Short sentences. Concrete over abstract — name the
Thursday afternoon, not "challenging moments." No exclamation marks, no emoji, no
hustle-culture register, no "unlock your potential." Assume the reader is a competent
adult who has been to a leadership workshop before and was unimpressed.

Confident but not grandiose. The claims are specific and modest; that is what makes
them credible next to competitors making enormous ones.
